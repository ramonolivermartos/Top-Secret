INTERNATIONAL COMMISSION FOR THE NON-LOCATION OF THE YETI

Six-monthly report of the Spanish Presidency. Declassified extract.

The yeti exists. Since 1961, custody of the subject has rotated every six months among the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council. The rotation was adopted so that no country could lose it twice in a row. Spain joined in 1986 owing to an error in the translation of Annex III, which the Commission chose not to correct, so as not to have to translate the correction as well.

Each presidency applies its own regulations, and the file shows it. In 2019 the Russian Federation issued a spravka, stamped in triplicate, certifying that the subject does not exist. The document is fully valid and has had one practical consequence: a non-existent being cannot be issued a passport, so it has travelled as freight ever since. The United Kingdom had previously devoted four years to a committee whose final report concluded that its existence "cannot be entirely ruled out". On that basis, HM Revenue & Customs classified it under the tariff heading for "articles of hair, not elsewhere specified".

The French presidency coincided with an air traffic controllers' strike that delayed the transfer from Chamonix by three weeks. The subject spent them in a café in Annecy while the Académie française debated whether "yéti" should take an accent, and it returned with a habit it has never lost: it now requests everything in writing. China commissioned a 1,400-page feasibility study on lending it to foreign zoos under the panda model. A third of the study is devoted to snowmen, since in Mandarin both are called 雪人. The United States funds seventy-one per cent of the programme and has classified the file so rigorously that the subject itself lacks the clearance required to read it.

The Spanish presidency began on 1 January with no funding whatsoever, as Spain's national budget was still being rolled over from a previous year. The subject was lodged in a holiday cottage in a village in the province of Soria with nine inhabitants. On the third day, true to its French habit, the yeti submitted a formal application to be entered on the municipal register. The mayor, who needed a tenth resident to keep the Tuesday doctor's surgery open, approved it that same morning.

The result is a regulatory conflict without precedent. According to Moscow, the yeti does not exist. According to the town hall, it resides at number 3, Calle Mayor, and is on the electoral roll. The Commission's legal service has ruled that both documents are valid and that the subject is, for legal purposes, a non-existent resident of Soria, a status it shares with much of a province that has spent years campaigning to prove it is still there.

The handover to the Chinese presidency, scheduled for 1 July, has been suspended. To leave the municipality, the yeti must first be removed from the register, and the earliest available appointment is in March 2027.

In sixty-five years, no intelligence service in the world has managed to locate it. The postman finds it every Tuesday.
